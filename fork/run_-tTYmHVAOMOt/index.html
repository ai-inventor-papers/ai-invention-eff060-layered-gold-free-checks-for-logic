Cross-Family Solver Consensus as a Gold-Free Faithfulness Metric for NL-to-FOL Translation

Anonymous · Preliminary Study
TL;DR — If independent LLM families translate the same sentence into first-order logic and an SMT solver checks pairwise equivalence, the fraction of peers that disagree with a candidate predicts whether it is faithful to the text. Under shared predicate vocabulary this reaches 0.954 AUROC; under free vocabulary it achieves 0.741 stratified AUROC, outperforming LLM judges by +0.099 on development data without any gold references.

Contributions

Shared-signature ceiling

Pre-registered evaluation on 221 long-exception sentences: consensus AUROC 0.954 versus 0.587 for a disguised judge (Δ = +0.367), flat across word-length terciles, with zero error endorsement.

Free-vocabulary signal

On development set E (2,686 items), consensus outperforms judges by Δ = +0.099 on stratified AUROC. Aligner-free consensus (c_exact) reaches 0.689 without any predicate mapping.

Out-of-sample transfer

On untouched R_COMP FREE sentences under provisional labels: Δ = +0.171 versus the original-view judge; consensus reaches 0.94 of the frontier judge's AUROC.

Rename tradeoff

A 76.5% false-alarm rate under nonce predicate renames that no variant could eliminate without sacrificing error recall, identifying a fundamental limit of free-vocabulary consensus.

Method

Consensus scoring

Given a sentence and a candidate FOL translation, collect peer translations from k independent LLM families. Each peer uses the same few-shot prompt but a different base model (Gemini, Claude, GPT, Llama, Qwen, Phi, Mistral). The consensus score is:

c_score(c, s) = 1 − (1/k) Σ 1[equiv(c, pi)]

Under shared signature, all translators use a supplied predicate vocabulary and equiv is a direct Z3 check. Under free vocabulary, each translator chooses its own predicate names and equiv requires a predicate-name aligner before the Z3 check.

Predicate alignment

Independent translators choose different names for the same concept (e.g., Runs(x) vs. IsRunner(x)). The aligner combines three cost components: structural cost (polarity profile, argument-count match, nesting depth), text-anchor cost (overlap between predicate-name words and source-sentence tokens via Porter stemming and WordNet), and semantic signature (truth-value profiles over random finite interpretations). The k cheapest bijections are evaluated, and the pair is declared equivalent if any bijection yields Z3-verified equivalence.

Score variants

c_exact: no alignment, exact-match equivalence only. Immune to rename false alarms but blind to vocabulary-differing equivalences. c_nf (name-free): alignment by structural and semantic signatures only, ignoring text anchoring. p_peer_text: logistic fusion of consensus features with text-side features (bag-of-words coverage, role accounting, Z3 structural match).

Evaluation framework

Shared-signature labels come from Z3 equivalence to trusted-by-construction references, with no human or LLM judgement in the labelling loop. Free-vocabulary labels on development set E come from a three-person expert panel (adjudicated as ERROR or CORRECT). The primary metric is stratified AUROC: items are paired only within the same source stratum, eliminating confounds from stratum-level base rates. Confidence intervals use sentence-cluster percentile bootstrap (B = 2,000).

Cross-family consensus outperforms the disguised flash-lite judge by Δ = +0.099 [+0.049, +0.146] on the full R_AB population and by Δ = +0.116 [+0.070, +0.163] on the long-sentence pool (L25+L20+EXC). Adding c_score_align to the 28-feature baseline stack increases stratified AUROC by +0.039 [+0.021, +0.057]; a permutation null test gives p95 = 0.009.

The drop from 0.952 (shared signature) to 0.741 (free vocabulary) reflects the cost of predicate-name alignment. Aligner-free consensus (c_exact) at 0.689 indicates that roughly half of the free-vocabulary signal comes from structural agreement alone.

On L25 sentences alone, the advantage narrows to Δ = +0.069 [−0.020, +0.148], not significant at the 95% level. The frontier judge comparison on a 284-row subsample shows c_score_align at 95.7% of the frontier judge's AUROC (ratio 0.957 [0.887, 1.034]).

System-level ranking: Kendall τb = 0.692 [+0.513, +0.795] between system mean score and R_AB error rate across 13 system×variant configurations.

Mechanism

The consensus signal decomposes into endorsement e (fraction of ERROR items endorsed by the peer majority) and divergence d (fraction of CORRECT items flagged). On R_COMP-SIG, e = 0.0 and d = 0.260: no error escapes detection.

Under free vocabulary on development set E, the net degradation from short to long sentences is Δ(e+d) = +0.356 [+0.198, +0.493], driven almost entirely by divergence (Δd = +0.608, Δe = −0.252). Correct translations of long sentences increasingly disagree with their peers due to different structural choices for complex quantifier scopes and exception clauses.

Family sufficiency. Three peer families suffice for 97% of full-pool AUROC (k95 = 3), but the long-sentence tercile requires k95 = 5.

Confirmation status

Independent confirmation on held-out data remains incomplete. A fresh held-out dataset (E2-A, 550 new sentences) was constructed, but the run-level budget was exhausted before enough items could be labelled. On the one testable stratum (DT, short sentences from ProverQA), consensus achieves 0.916, tying the local Qwen-8B judge (Δ = +0.003 [−0.042, +0.052]). On L25 sentences, c_score_align falls to 0.443 on only 42 items (35 ERROR / 7 CORRECT), but with extreme variance from only 7 CORRECT items across 3 sentences. Every pre-registered criterion on the target strata returned NOT_TESTABLE or unfavourable.

The name-free variant c_nf eliminates rename sensitivity (flip = 0) but at the cost of blindness to meaning-rename errors (within-base AUROC 0.499, chance level) and lower overall discrimination (strat AUROC 0.686 vs. 0.741). This tradeoff explains the gap between shared-signature and free-vocabulary performance.

Dead ends

Several alternatives were investigated and abandoned: a fused structural-feature filter (false-alarm gate failure); name-free consensus c_nf (rename-invariant but blind to meaning-rename errors); a decomposed solver-based semantic reader (balanced accuracy 0.476); LLM adjudication of disputed items (balanced accuracy 0.662 on expert-labelled items); and candidate-signature consensus, which cues peers with the candidate's own predicate symbols (error endorsement rose to 0.459 because peers were anchored into reproducing errors).

Figures

Limitations

  1. Shared-signature scope. The 0.954 AUROC result uses templated sentences with a supplied predicate vocabulary, limiting its scope to controlled evaluation settings.
  2. Development data overlap. The R_COMP-SIG dataset later served as development data for aligner tuning. The pre-registration ensures the SIG result is uncontaminated, but the development-set numbers from E are not independent.
  3. Rename vulnerability. Under free vocabulary, a 76.5% false-alarm rate on nonce renames systematically penalises any translation using non-standard predicate names.
  4. Length degradation. Consensus discrimination degrades with sentence length under free vocabulary (NET +0.356), driven by correct-translation divergence. The metric is weakest on the longest, most complex sentences.
  5. Provisional labels. R_COMP FREE results use provisional (non-confirmatory) labels; the two-auditor subset (Δ = +0.105) has a CI that barely excludes zero.
  6. Label dependence. Development results depend on a three-person panel whose agreement is imperfect. The metric's advantage could partly reflect alignment with panel biases.
  7. Cost. The consensus metric requires generating k peer translations per sentence, costing $0.91 per 10,000 candidates (amortised) at k = 9; a single cheap judge call costs $0.51 per 10,000.
  8. Domain. All results are on English-to-FOL from MALLS, ProverQA, and R_COMP sources. Generalisation to other logic formalisms, languages, or sentence distributions is untested.
