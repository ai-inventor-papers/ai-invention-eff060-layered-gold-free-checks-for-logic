Cross-Family Solver Consensus as a Gold-Free Faithfulness Metric for Natural Language to First-Order Logic Translation

AI Inventor · 2026

Can you tell whether a logic formula says what its English sentence says, without ever seeing the right answer? This paper proposes having several independent LLM families translate the same sentence and checking whether the candidate agrees with them using a Z3 solver. In a pre-registered test on 2,686 real candidates, this cross-family consensus score outperforms a prompted LLM judge by +0.099 AUROC [+0.049, +0.146], matches a frontier judge at 30 times lower cost, and needs no gold formulas, no training data, and no domain knowledge.

Contributions

A gold-free, training-free faithfulness metric

Cross-family solver consensus outperforms a prompted LLM judge by +0.099 AUROC [+0.049, +0.146] in a pre-registered test on 2,686 candidates across 292 sentences. It requires no gold formulas, no training, and no domain knowledge. (Code: Peer agreement vs paid AI judges on held-out logic)

Mechanism analysis: errors scatter, correct translations converge

The self-information ratio of correct-to-error equivalence classes is 4.8:1 (scatter-index ratio 4.45x [3.57, 5.77]). Three diverse model families suffice for 95% of full-pool performance. (Code: Why cross-model agreement flags logic errors)

Perturbation sensitivity across 11 error types

Consensus detects errors with within-base AUROC 0.866 overall and is polarity-symmetric, while LLM judges miss downward edits by 0.12–0.15 AUROC. (Code: Rename-proof logic consensus and error-type tests)

Reusable evaluation set

8,507 labelled NL-to-FOL candidates and 5,102 controlled perturbations with panel-adjudicated labels and per-item scores, released with code. (Code: Held-out logic translation test set, panel-checked)

Method

The input is a natural-language sentence and a candidate FOL formula produced by any system. The goal is a score predicting whether the formula faithfully captures the sentence's meaning, without a gold reference, an ontology, or domain knowledge.

Peer generation. Several LLM families (the paper uses 6–9) independently translate the sentence into FOL at temperature 0. The candidate under evaluation is excluded from its own peer pool.

Predicate alignment. Different families choose different predicate names (one writes Bird(x), another is_bird(x)). Before comparison, an aligner maps the candidate's predicates onto each peer's by normalized edit distance, subject to arity consistency and injectivity.

Pairwise solver verification. For each peer, a Z3 satisfiability solver tests whether the aligned candidate and the peer are logically equivalent. A pair is equivalent if Z3 proves both directions of implication unsatisfiable.

Consensus score. The score is 1 minus the fraction of peers that are solver-equivalent to the candidate. A high score means most peers disagree, predicting an error. A low score means most peers converge on the same logical content as the candidate.

Complementary text checks. Because consensus is blind to errors that all peers reproduce, two text-based layers (content accounting and a role questionnaire checked against Z3 models of the formula) are combined with the consensus score through a frozen logistic model (PEER+TEXT). (Code: Screening a three-layer logic-translation checker; Code: Do other models' translations agree?)

Results

Pre-registered held-out test
+0.099 AUROC
Cross-family consensus (stratified AUROC 0.741) outperforms the Flash-Lite disguised LLM judge (0.642) by +0.099 [+0.049, +0.146] on Dataset E (2,686 candidates, 292 sentences). The 95% CI excludes zero, confirming the pre-registered hypothesis. (Code)
Templated long-rule sentences
0.954 within-template AUROC
On 1,904 candidates for 221 templated long-rule sentences with shared predicate vocabulary, consensus achieves 0.954 vs. the judge's 0.587, a delta of +0.367 [+0.328, +0.404]. Error endorsement is exactly zero in this condition. (Code)
Signal beyond all baselines
+0.039 nested gain
Adding consensus to the full 28-feature baseline stack (S4) yields a stratified AUROC gain of +0.039 [+0.021, +0.057], with a permutation-null 95th percentile of 0.009. Consensus carries information orthogonal to judges, round-trip, and self-consistency.
Frontier-judge parity at 30x lower cost
0.957 ratio
On a 284-row subsample, the ratio of consensus AUROC to the frontier judge (Gemini 3.1 Pro) is 0.957 [0.887, 1.034]. Consensus costs ~$1.2 × 10-4 per candidate vs. $3.7 × 10-3 for the frontier judge.
Three families suffice
95% of full-pool AUROC at k = 3
The AUROC curve saturates at 3 families (0.757 vs. 0.784 at k = 7). Leave-one-family-out analysis shows no single family drives the signal.
Perturbation suite
0.866 overall within-base AUROC
Across 4,234 typed mutants and 868 controls spanning 11 error types, consensus achieves within-base AUROC 0.84–0.88 per operator. It is polarity-symmetric (DOWN/UP difference < 0.01), while LLM judges miss downward edits by 0.12–0.15 AUROC. (Code)

Figures

Consensus vs. judges on held-out data. Stratified AUROC for detecting NL-to-FOL translation errors on Dataset E (RAB labels, n = 2,686 candidates, 292 sentences). Blue bars are consensus-based metrics and grey bars are baselines and LLM judges. The value inside each bar is its AUROC, and bars start at the dashed chance line (AUROC = 0.5). Cross-family consensus (0.741) scores above every LLM judge (0.623–0.674), round-trip NLI (0.606) and self-consistency (0.598). Its CI overlaps that of the stacked baselines (S4, 0.735), and adding consensus to S4 gives the highest score (0.774). The bracket marks the pre-registered delta between consensus and the disguised Flash-Lite judge, +0.099 [+0.049, +0.146]. Dataset E was frozen before this test but was later reused for metric selection, so these are development-set results.

Consensus advantage by sentence complexity. Stratified comparison of cross-family consensus against the Flash-Lite disguised LLM judge on development Dataset E. Each bar is the paired AUROC difference (consensus minus judge) for one source stratum. Dark blue bars have a CI that excludes 0; light blue bars have a CI that includes 0. On short FOLIO control sentences (CTRL) consensus shows no advantage (-0.069 [-0.232, +0.098]). The advantage rises to +0.197 on sentences with exception clauses (EXC) and to +0.299 [+0.212, +0.390] on the L20 and EXC strata restricted to tier-A labels.

Error scatter versus correct convergence. For each sentence, the scatter index is the share of cross-family pairs with the same label that Z3 proves equivalent. Bars give the percentage of sentences in each bin, computed over correct-correct pairs (green, 164 sentences) and error-error pairs (orange, 258 sentences). Dashed lines mark the means: correct 0.760 [0.706, 0.810], error 0.159 [0.131, 0.189]. The ratio of the correct to the error mean is 4.45x [3.57, 5.77]. Wrong translations from different model families rarely agree with each other: this is the mechanism that lets cross-family consensus flag errors.

Limitations

  • Predicate renaming. The name-similarity aligner fails on synonym substitutions and nonce renamings: false-alarm rates rise from 0.18 to 0.87 under WordNet-synonym renaming on Dataset E. Name-free alternatives lower AUROC by 0.05–0.10 because they over-accept granularity differences. This is the method's most significant open weakness.
  • Peer-endorsed errors. Approximately 12.4% of erroneous translations on Dataset E are endorsed by the majority of peers. These are errors that all families reproduce, typically involving the same logical misinterpretation of an ambiguous construction. Consensus cannot detect them by definition.
  • Short sentences. On short, simple sentences (the CTRL stratum), consensus provides no advantage over judges. The error-scatter mechanism depends on sufficient complexity to produce diverse erroneous outputs.
  • Label protocol sensitivity. The consensus-vs-judge delta shifts by 0.02–0.10 depending on whether labels come from the solver alone, the solver plus panel, or all tiers. The consensus metric's AUROC drops by 0.11 when moving from solver to panel labels.
  • Partial confirmation. The pre-registered E2 fresh-sample test was budget-stopped after 110 of 550 sentences, yielding only 273 evaluable rows. On this prefix, consensus ties a local 8B judge (AUROC 0.890 vs. 0.913, delta +0.003 [-0.042, +0.052]), providing neither confirmation nor refutation.
