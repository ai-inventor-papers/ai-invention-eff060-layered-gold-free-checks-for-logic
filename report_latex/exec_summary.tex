\section*{Goal}

This research investigated whether a candidate first-order logic (FOL) formula can be scored for faithfulness to its source natural-language sentence \emph{without} a reference (gold) formula.
The run explored three metric families---deterministic formula checks (FOL-Triage), cross-family solver consensus, and text-based alignment---over five iterations, benchmarked against LLM judges and structural baselines on two public datasets (FOLIO, MALLS) with solver-verified labels.
The bar to beat was a disguised cheap LLM judge (Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite, AUROC 0.777).

\section*{What was tried}

\textbf{Iteration 1.}
Four experiments on the Logic-LM frozen screen ($n=546$): FOL-Triage (deterministic lint, bag-of-words, and questionnaire layers), cross-system consensus via z3 equivalence among 9 LLM families, a full baselines suite (judges, round-trip NLI, structural metrics), and a summary comparison of discrimination vs.\ rename invariance.

\textbf{Iteration 2.}
Five experiments on development set E ($n=2{,}686$ rows, 292 sentences): PEER+TEXT fusion of consensus with text layers, re-evaluation under alternative label regimes, name-free consensus, and baselines.
Also attempted: B2 decomposed z3 reader (DROPPED) and R\_ADJ adjudicator
(DROPPED).

\textbf{Iteration 3.}
Confirmation on development set E with the head-on API bar test (T1), long composed sentences from R\_COMP, PERTURB sensitivity/rename tests, and mechanism verification (hypothesis testing on error endorsement and complexity slope).

\textbf{Iteration 4.}
Candidate-Signature Consensus (CSC): tested whether giving peers the candidate's own symbols improves discrimination.
Also: CSC on PERTURB (budget exhausted), E2 confirmation set construction (partial), R\_COMP FREE name-free labels, and vocabulary analysis.

\textbf{Iteration 5.}
Freeze-and-screen of five V0 variants, Gloss-Gated (GG) name-free checker, and three confirmation arms (all NOT\_TESTABLE due to budget exhaustion).

The cross-family solver consensus metric (c\_score\_align) is the strongest gold-free signal discovered.
It beats the cheap judge by a statistically significant margin on both the frozen screen and the development set.
Adding text-accounting layers (PEER+TEXT) provides no significant gain over c\_score\_align alone once stratum-composition confounds are removed.
The consensus advantage is largest on long, exception-bearing sentences: on R\_A L20+EXC, c\_score\_align reaches 0.860 while the judge falls to 0.554 ($\Delta$ +0.306 [+0.232, +0.376]).

The fundamental tradeoff is between discrimination and rename invariance.
Consensus achieves the highest AUROC (0.866) but has a RENAME flip rate of 0.765 because z3 equivalence is sensitive to predicate names.
FOL-Triage is nearly rename-invariant (flip rate 0.013) but sits below the judge bar.
CSC attempted to fix the rename problem by sharing the candidate's vocabulary with peers; the anchoring effect ($e = 0.459$) dominated the convergence benefit ($d$ reduction $-0.184$), producing a net negative.
GG attempted to bypass names entirely with a gloss-gated meaning matcher; it achieved low error endorsement ($e = 0.081$) but very high correct-row divergence ($d = 0.629$).

\section*{Best result}

Cross-family solver consensus (c\_score\_align): \textbf{stratified AUROC 0.774} [0.739, 0.808] on development set E (R\_AB PRIMARY, 354 rows, 144 sentences), confirmed in iteration 3, Test T1.
It beats the flash-lite judge by $\Delta$ +0.099 [+0.049, +0.146] (paired, stratified).
On long sentences with exceptions, the advantage grows to $\Delta$ +0.306.
The metric costs \$0.000525 per candidate (30$\times$ cheaper than the judge) and requires peer translations from $\geq$3 independent model families plus z3 pairwise equivalence checking.
Its main limitation is non-invariance under predicate renaming (flip rate 0.765 under RENAME); all attempts to fix this (name-free consensus, CSC, GG) degraded discrimination.

\section*{Open questions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Rename invariance.} No variant achieved both high AUROC and low rename sensitivity. Can a normalisation layer (canonical naming, signature-free equivalence) close the gap without anchoring?
\item \textbf{Confirmation on held-out data.} The E2 confirmation set was constructed (550 sentences) but labelling was incomplete due to budget exhaustion. All three iteration-5 confirmation arms are NOT\_TESTABLE.
\item \textbf{Flash-lite bar on E.} The primary bar (flash-lite judge) is UNTESTABLE on the full development set; only 20 items were scored before API key exhaustion. The local Qwen3-8B fallback was used instead.
\item \textbf{Frontier judge interaction.} Under adjudicated labels, the frontier judge advantage over the cheap judge is +0.166 (significant). Whether consensus still beats the frontier judge on adjudicated labels is untested.
\item \textbf{Cost of diversity.} The peer pool requires $\geq$3 model families. Whether cheaper or fewer peers suffice without degrading AUROC is unexplored.
\item \textbf{Error typing.} Consensus detects COMPOUND and polarity errors well (recall 0.55, 0.53) but has near-zero recall on MEANING\_RENAME and PEER\_ENDORSED errors. Can error-type-specific signals be combined?
\end{enumerate}
