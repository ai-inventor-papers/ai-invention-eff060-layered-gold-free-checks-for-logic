\section{Headline}

Cross-family solver consensus---the fraction of independently generated peer translations that are \emph{not} z3-equivalent to a candidate FOL formula---outperforms prompted LLM judges for detecting NL-to-FOL translation errors. On a pre-registered, held-out test on 2,686 real LLM-generated candidates (development dataset~E, R\_AB labels, 292 sentences), the consensus metric \texttt{c\_score\_align} achieves a stratified AUROC of 0.741, exceeding the best cheap LLM judge (gemini-2.5-flash-lite, disguised) at 0.642: a difference of $+0.099$ [95\% CI: $+0.049$, $+0.146$] (Iteration~3, gen\_art\_experiment\_6). Consensus matches a frontier judge at roughly 30$\times$ lower cost, adds signal beyond a stack of 28 baselines, and requires no gold formulas, no training, and no domain knowledge.

The advantage is confirmed on development data but \textbf{not yet on independent confirmation data}: every confirmation arm (E2-A, E2-B, R\_COMP FREE) was budget-stopped before reaching testability.

\section{Goal}

This run investigates gold-free metrics that predict whether a candidate first-order logic formula faithfully captures its source natural-language sentence, without a reference formula, ontology, or domain knowledge. Gold FOL annotations are expensive, non-unique, and unreliable (approximately 42\% of entries in FOLIO and MALLS contain incorrect formalisations). The evaluation is a meta-evaluation: we build labelled sets from public benchmarks whose gold has been audited and measure each metric's ability to discriminate correct translations from errors.

\section{What Was Tried}

\textbf{Iteration~1.} Screened four metric families head-to-head on a frozen Logic-LM FOLIO screen: FOL-Triage (three-layer cascade), round-trip verification, cross-system consensus, and a disguised cheap LLM judge as the bar. Built development dataset~E (700 sentences, 8,507 rows).

\textbf{Iteration~2.} Confirmed the iteration-1 ordering on dataset~E; tested PEER+TEXT fusion of consensus and text-based checks; constructed the PERTURB typed perturbation suite (4,234 mutants, 868 controls); re-evaluated all metrics under alternative label regimes.

\textbf{Iteration~3.} Ran the head-on API bar test (T1 CONFIRMED): consensus beats the flash-lite judge by $+0.099$ AUROC. Tested on R\_COMP long composed sentences (SIG within-template AUROC 0.954 vs judge 0.587). Ran per-operator PERTURB sensitivity and rename tradeoff tests. Verified 48/48 numerical claims from iteration~2.

\textbf{Iteration~4.} Tested Candidate-Signature Consensus (CSC): a provisional negative result due to anchoring ($e = 0.459$ vs $0.173$ for free peers). Constructed the E2 confirmation dataset (550 sentences, partial). Produced name-free labels for R\_COMP FREE candidates.

\textbf{Iteration~5.} Attempted confirmation on three fronts (E2-A, E2-B, R\_COMP FREE); all three budget-stopped before testability. Froze V0 as the shipped metric (no variant V1--V5 met the selection threshold). Tested the Gloss-Gated (GG) name-free checker: failed its development gate. Corrected 15 paper-vs-file mismatches.

\section{Open Questions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{No confirmation.} Every confirmation arm was budget-stopped. The headline $+0.099$ AUROC advantage is on development data only.
  \item \textbf{Rename tradeoff unsolved.} V0 has RENAME\_NONCE false-alarm rate 0.765 on E and 1.0 on E2. No variant achieves both low rename FA and high error recall.
  \item \textbf{Domain transfer untested.} All evaluation uses FOLIO and MALLS-derived data.
  \item \textbf{Label noise.} Panel majority accuracy on expert-curated errors is 0.727; R\_COMP FREE audit shows 13--28\% label contamination.
  \item \textbf{Complexity advantage inconclusive.} The consensus advantage does not demonstrably grow with sentence complexity (M3 DISCONFIRMED on the length-slope test).
\end{enumerate}
